## 9. Groupon and the daily-deal industry — the merchant side, which the plan has not costed

**This is the closest analogue to the voucher-funded model, and the one with real peer-reviewed evidence.** Groupon's user side worked. Its merchant side did not — and the merchant side is where YourTal's revenue comes from.

### 9.1 The academic record on merchant outcomes

| Study | Sample | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| [Dholakia 2010, Rice](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1696327) (Groupon only) | 150 businesses, 19 US cities, 13 categories, deals Jun 2009–Aug 2010 | Profitable for **66%**, unprofitable for **32%**. Returned to buy again **at full price**: **31%** (profitable merchants) vs **13%** (unprofitable). Spent beyond face value: **50% vs 25%**. **"More than 40% said they would not run such a promotion again."** Restaurants fared worst; salons/spas best. ([Rice release](https://phys.org/news/2010-09-groupon-beneficial-consumers-businesses.html), [ClickZ](https://clickz.com/study-groupon-unprofitable-for-a-third-of-businesses/54466/)) |
| [Dholakia 2011](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Papers.cfm?abstract_id=1863466) (5 deal sites) | 324 businesses, 23 markets, Aug 2009–Mar 2011 | 55.5% profitable / 26.6% lost money / 17.9% breakeven. **~80% of deal users were new customers — but only 19.9% ever returned to purchase at full price**; only 35.9% spent beyond face value; **21.7% never redeemed**. Only **48.1%** would run another deal; restaurants/bars **35.9%**. ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/06/110614154548.htm), [ClickZ](https://clickz.com/only-20-of-daily-deals-users-come-back-for-full-purchases/50967/)) |
| [Dholakia 2012](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2091655) (3 waves) | 641 SMBs, Apr 2011–May 2012 | **Merchant survivorship through seven deals ≈ 30%** — 41% for sub-$500K-revenue businesses vs **15% for larger businesses**. Fewer than half profit on their *first* deal; >75% profit by their seventh. ([Rice News](https://news2.rice.edu/2012/07/05/daily-deal-industry-shows-no-evidence-of-slowing-down-2/)) |
| [Edelman, Jaffe & Kominers](https://www.benedelman.org/publications/groupon-2014-03-01.pdf), HBS WP 11-063 → *Marketing Letters* 27(1):39–53 (2016), [DOI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11002-014-9289-y) | theoretical model | Vouchers work via price discrimination **only if voucher users have systematically lower valuations** than regular customers. If the populations are identical, **vouchers reduce profits**; *"if vouchers provide discounts to many long-time customers, vouchers could sharply reduce profits."* The advertising benefit accrues to **newer, less well-known firms** — those least able to absorb the cost. *"As voucher services grow, they must rely more on advertising benefits rather than on price discrimination benefits."* |
| [Kumar & Rajan, *MIT Sloan Mgmt Review* 53(4), 2012](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-perils-of-social-coupon-campaigns/) ([PDF](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/17a163f6ab.pdf)); peer-reviewed companion [*JAMS* 40(1):120–136](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-011-0283-0) | 3 US businesses, full P&L | Ethnic restaurant: first-month loss **$7,313**, **–$14 profit per new customer**, **18 months to recoup**. Car wash: **–$6,300**, –$17/customer, 15 months. Beauty salon: **–$11,760**, **–$39 per new customer**, **98+ months (>8 years) to recoup**. A **1% reduction in *existing* customers receiving coupons cut the salon's shortfall by $495** — cannibalisation dominated the loss. |
| [Byers, Mitzenmacher & Zervas, WSDM 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.1530) and [ACM EC 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.2369) | 16,692 Groupon deals, 20 US cities, 5,472 matched to Yelp, vs 7.1M Yelp reviews | *"Average rating scores from reviewers who mention daily deals are **10% lower** than scores of their peers."* Review volume **rises** while average rating **falls**, with *"a striking discontinuity … concurrent with the offer date"* and further drops at **180- and 360-day lags — the two commonest expiry dates**. Deal users are **not** harsher reviewers generally — *"they are more moderate in the ratings they provide"* ([SIGecom Exchanges 11(2)](https://www.sigecom.org/exchanges/volume_11/2/BYERS.pdf)) — so the drop reflects real experience, not cohort grumpiness. |
| [Zervas, Byers & Mitzenmacher, "Does a Daily Deal Promotion Signal a Distressed Business?"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.1694) | 985 Chicago deal merchants vs 32,424 Yelp businesses, survival analysis | **Positive, significant correlation between the latent factors that make a business run a deal and those that contribute to failure** (ρ≈0.28 restaurants/bars). **Honest caveat:** raw closure rates were *lower* for deal merchants (8.3% vs 17.7%), so this is a conditional finding, not "Groupon kills businesses." ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2012/11/12/114843/groupon-daily-deals-and-the-complex-question-of-business-failure/)) |

**Groupon has never disclosed a merchant churn or retention number** in any 10-K, 10-Q or S-1. Merchant attrition appears only as a qualitative risk factor — *"we may experience attrition in our merchants in the ordinary course of business…"* ([FY2015 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1490281/000149028116000087/groupon201510-k.htm)); merchants are *"largely small businesses that are highly sensitive to economic shocks"* ([FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1490281/000162828026016429/grpn-20251231.htm)). It stopped reporting merchant counts entirely after the IPO. **A company that stops reporting a metric is telling you what that metric did.**

Groupon's counter-claim (>95% of ~3,000 polled merchants would work with them again — [Retail Customer Experience](https://www.retailcustomerexperience.com/articles/groupon-responds-95-percent-of-merchants-would-do-it-again/)) is self-reported and unaudited. Independent surveys point the other way: ~52% of merchants not planning another deal within six months (Susquehanna/Yipit, Jan 2012, n=100 of 400 — [TIME](https://business.time.com/2012/01/04/groupon-remorse-most-merchants-wont-run-daily-deals-over-next-six-months/)); 82% of 1,087 small businesses said they have not and will not run one (Manta, Nov 2012 — [TIME](https://business.time.com/2012/11/16/is-the-daily-deal-model-dying-a-slow-death/)). *Both TIME figures were obtained via search extraction from a host with a broken TLS chain — treat as weaker than the academic set.*

### 9.3 Australia: the regulator has already litigated the exact pitch YourTal plans to make

In December 2013 the Federal Court fined **Scoopon A$1 million** under the Australian Consumer Law. Among the findings: Scoopon made false or misleading representations **to businesses that there was no cost or risk in running a deal**, and **to a business that 30% of vouchers sold would not be redeemed, when there was no reasonable basis for that representation.** The Court added a two-year injunction and a community-service order to run an ACL seminar for other group-buying sites. ([ACCC outcome](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/scoopon-to-pay-1-million-for-misleading-both-businesses-and-consumers), [ACCC original action, 5 Jul 2013](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-takes-action-against-scoopon-alleging-it-misled-both-consumers-and-businesses), [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2013-12-17/scoopon-fined-241-million-for-misleading-customers/5162442))

**Read that against YourTal's merchant deck.** "Your real cost is only COGS because many vouchers go unredeemed" is *precisely the representation Scoopon was fined for making without a reasonable basis.* Any breakage figure in an Australian merchant pitch must be evidenced from YourTal's own redemption data, documented and updated — or it is an ACL exposure from day one. See [`09-points-economy-and-redemption.md`](09-points-economy-and-redemption.md) §breakage.

### 9.4 Eight lessons for YourTal

1. **~80% new customers and ~20% full-price return is the *good* case.** Model merchant ROI at a 20% return rate, not a hoped-for 50%.
2. **Restaurants are the worst category** (44.2% profitable, 35.9% would repeat) and the category most likely to say yes first. Adverse selection runs against you at merchant acquisition.
3. **Cannibalisation, not acquisition, kills merchant P&L.** If YourTal's audience contains a merchant's existing customers, the campaign converts full-price revenue into discounted revenue. Require exclusion of known customers, and measure it.
4. **Merchant survivorship through seven campaigns was ~30%, and 15% for larger businesses.** YourTal's revenue depends on repeat campaigns; assume you must re-sell most of the merchant base every few campaigns.
5. **Ratings drop ~10% after a deal, with a second dip at expiry.** A 30-minute video plus quiz *amplifies* this: the user has invested half an hour and arrives with elevated expectations.
6. **Payback periods run to years.** The salon took 8+. YourTal charges cash *on top of* voucher COGS — so the merchant's payback is structurally **worse** than Groupon's, not better. §2.5 of [`01`](01-strategy-and-economics.md) compares YourTal to Meta CAC; it should compare to **Groupon merchant payback**, which is the honest benchmark.
7. **Instrument merchant second-campaign rate from campaign #1** and treat it as the company's primary KPI, above MAU.
8. **Do not put an unevidenced non-redemption rate in front of an Australian merchant.**

## 10. Plenti (American Express) — the pooled store, exactly as YourTal proposes it

**This is the closest structural analogue to YourTal's "earn at merchant A, spend at merchant B" pool, and it is a failure.**

| Fact | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Model | US coalition loyalty launched by **American Express**, 2015: earn at one partner, redeem at any partner | [Plenti (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenti) |
| Launch partners | Macy's, Rite Aid, ExxonMobil, AT&T, Hulu, Nationwide, Enterprise, Expedia, Chili's | [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/report-amexs-plenti-program-faces-rocky-future-as-macys-other-retailers/516397/) |
| Exodus | AT&T left late 2017; Chili's followed; **Direct Energy, Hulu, Nationwide, Enterprise and Expedia all announced departure on New Year's Day 2018**; Macy's left Q1 2018 | same |
| Shut down | **10 July 2018** — three years after launch | [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/loyalty-and-rewards-news/2018/amex-pulls-plug-on-plenti-rewards-program/) |
| Redemption value | **$10 per 1,000 points** — cited as a primary reason retailers abandoned it | [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/report-amexs-plenti-program-faces-rocky-future-as-macys-other-retailers/516397/) |
| Governance | three different programme leaders in three years | same |

**The killer mechanic — read this twice.** A Macy's source described the value flow as **"asymmetrical": consumers accumulated points at staple, high-frequency partners (grocery, fuel) and redeemed them at Macy's, which offered higher-quality goods.** The result: *"it didn't drive new traffic into stores"* ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/report-amexs-plenti-program-faces-rocky-future-as-macys-other-retailers/516397/)). Commentators describe aligning coalition partners across categories as *"herding cats"* ([Coleman Insights](https://colemaninsights.com/coleman-insights-blog/why-the-plenti-loyalty-program-failed)).

**That is precisely YourTal's pooled voucher store.** In a pool, points are *issued* by whoever has the cheapest attention to buy and *redeemed* against whoever has the most desirable inventory. **Those are never the same merchants.** The desirable merchants become a settlement liability funded by less desirable merchants' campaigns, receive footfall they did not buy, and leave.

**Lesson for YourTal.** The clearing house in [`07-coalition-clearing-and-commerce.md`](07-coalition-clearing-and-commerce.md) is not an implementation detail — **it is the single largest failure risk in the product, and American Express could not make it work in the world's richest market.** If YourTal cannot answer *"what stops the best merchant from being everyone's redemption target and nobody's campaign funder"*, the pool is a subsidy from weak brands to strong ones, and the strong brands will still leave — because footfall from a voucher they did not fund is footfall they did not want.

## 11. Regulatory precedent you cannot ignore: TikTok Lite Rewards

**In April 2024 TikTok launched almost exactly YourTal's mechanic:** earn points for watching videos and liking content, **exchange points for third-party vouchers** or in-app currency.

| Event | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| EU Commission opens DSA proceedings over TikTok Lite rewards in France and Spain; signals intent to suspend | 22 Apr 2024 | [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-opens-proceedings-against-tiktok-under-dsa-regarding-launch-tiktok-lite-france-and-spain) |
| TikTok voluntarily suspends the programme | late Apr 2024 | [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/04/24/tiktok-lite-rewards-app-european-union-eu-investigation/) |
| Commission makes withdrawal **legally binding and permanent**; TikTok barred from launching any circumventing programme | 5 Aug 2024 | [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2024-09-18/european-union-tiktok-agrees-with-commission-to-withdraw-rewards-program/) |
| Stated grounds | risk of **addictive behaviour**, especially to minors; no prior systemic-risk assessment. TikTok is also barred from relaunching it under another name; breach is sanctionable immediately at **up to 6% of global annual turnover** with no fresh investigation | [Harneys](https://www.harneys.com/our-blogs/regulatory/tiktok-permanently-ends-tiktok-lite-rewards-programme-in-eu-for-dsa-compliance/), [BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/ue-une-application-de-tiktok-retir%C3%A9e-d%C3%A9finitivement-en-raison-des-risques-potentiels-graves-pour-la-sant%C3%A9-mentale-des-utilisateurs/) |

**Lesson for YourTal.** A major regulator has already ruled that *this exact mechanic* is a consumer-harm risk requiring a prior risk assessment. Australia — which has moved hard on online safety and age assurance — is not a safe assumption. Do the risk assessment **before** launch, gate minors hard, cap daily earn, and treat "engagement maximisation via reward loops" as a regulated design pattern rather than a growth lever. Cross-reference [`03-regulatory-and-risk.md`](03-regulatory-and-risk.md).

## 12. The time-value arithmetic nobody in this category survived

| Benchmark | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Australian national minimum wage from 1 Jul 2026 | **A$26.44/hour** | [Fair Work Commission](https://www.fwc.gov.au/work-conditions/minimum-wages-and-conditions/national-minimum-wage) |
| Jakarta minimum wage 2026 | Rp5,729,876/month ÷ 173 hrs ≈ **Rp33,120/hour** *(our arithmetic)* | [Kepgub 1142/2025](https://m.beritajakarta.id/read/149722/naik-617-persen-ump-dki-jakarta-2026-ditetapkan-rp5729876), [PP 36/2021 divisor](https://mekari.com/blog/cara-menghitung-upah-per-jam/) |
| Perk TV realised rate | a phone running **all night** ≈ **55 cents** | [Frugal for Less](https://www.frugalforless.com/perk-tv-review/) |
| Successor rewarded-video apps, realistic | **$1–3/hour** | same |
| Crypto learn-to-earn, typical | **$5–15/hour** — and the flagship was discontinued | [EarnifyHub](https://earnifyhub.com/blog/crypto/learn-to-earn-crypto-platforms-2026) (secondary source; indicative only) |

A 30-minute YourTal campaign paying an IDR 50,000 voucher implies **IDR 100,000/hour**, which genuinely clears the Indonesian bar by a wide margin. That is the plan's real strength and it should be stress-tested, not assumed. **In Australia the same 30 minutes for an A$5 voucher is A$10/hour — 38% of minimum wage.** Every product above that offered a below-wage rate ended up serving two cohorts only: the very low-opportunity-cost, and the automated. Neither is a cohort advertisers pay a premium for.

## 13. The pattern

### 13.1 What the survivors have that the failures did not

| # | The rule | Survivors | Failures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The reward is funded by a transaction, not by an impression.** Prodege pays out of a commission that only exists because someone bought something; Mistplay out of IAP revenue a publisher can see on its own ledger; Fetch out of a brand's trade budget against a scanned receipt. | Prodege, Mistplay, Fetch | Viggle, Perk, Brave, StepN paid a **fixed** cost per view regardless of outcome. Fixed cost against variable revenue is a structurally losing position. |
| 2 | **The real product is data or a measurable action, sold into a research/performance budget — not attention sold into a media budget.** In a media budget you compete with Meta on price. In a research budget you compete with Kantar on quality, which is a far easier fight. | Prodege sells to Walmart, Clorox, DoorDash; Fetch licenses receipt data | Viggle and Perk sold impressions and were price-takers against a market with infinite supply |
| 3 | **The advertised thing converts fast and is attributable.** A game monetises in days. A restaurant visit does not. | Mistplay | Everyone whose advertiser could not see the outcome |
| 4 | **Rewards are variable and downside-protected.** Survivors can cut payout without breaking the product because payout is a *share*. | Prodege, Mistplay | Viggle's points liability was fixed and could only be reduced by re-estimating breakage |
| 5 | **They consolidated rather than competed.** Prodege bought InboxDollars, Pollfish, BitBurst, AdGate. Perk bought Viggle and AppRedeem — and still died, because it bought *attention* assets, not *demand* assets. | Prodege | Perk |
| 6 | **They never needed a coalition.** Nobody in the survivor column runs a pooled, cross-merchant points economy. **The only serious attempt at that, by American Express, died in three years.** | — | Plenti |

### 13.2 The specific things this evidence says about YourTal

| The plan says | The evidence says |
|---|---|
| Video is the engagement surface; direct-sold long-form is the revenue engine | Viggle and Perk both sold long-form-ish engagement and both ended at <1x revenue. Long-form does not change the arithmetic; **funding source does** |
| Merchants fund rewards from COGS, so cost is low | Kumar & Rajan measured actual merchant P&L: **–$14 to –$39 profit per new customer, 15 to 98 months payback.** COGS is not the merchant's whole cost — cannibalisation and service load are |
| The voucher redemption is the conversion event, which Meta cannot sell | True and genuinely strong. But only **19.9% of Groupon redeemers ever came back at full price**, so the *second* purchase — the one that makes CAC worth paying — mostly does not happen |
| Points earned at merchant A are spendable at merchant B | **This is Plenti.** Amex's partners left because points accrued cheaply at staples and were redeemed at premium merchants who *"didn't [get] new traffic into stores"* |
| Quiz questions verify attention and deter fraud | Perk Pop Quiz was quiz-gated and was farmed anyway at ~55 cents per phone per night |
| Investor-funded voucher inventory breaks the cold start | It breaks the cold start and **hides the only signal that matters** — whether a merchant will fund a second campaign with their own money. Subsidised inventory buys you users you cannot price and merchants you cannot qualify |

### 13.3 Is this category structurally hard? Yes. Say it plainly.

**Attention-for-rewards is a structurally unprofitable business as a standalone, and the evidence is one-sided.** Every pure-play died or retreated; every survivor is a data or performance-marketing business wearing a rewards app as a customer-acquisition skin. The coalition layer YourTal adds on top has its own independent failure record, and the merchant-funded voucher layer under it has a peer-reviewed literature showing the merchant loses money on the first campaign and does not return.

**That is not the same as saying YourTal cannot work.** Three things in the plan are genuinely different from every failure above, and each is testable cheaply:

1. **The Indonesian time-value gap is real.** IDR 100,000/hour of effective reward against local wages clears the effort threshold by a margin no US or Australian product ever had. This is the strongest thing in the plan. **Test it first and test it honestly** — including whether users will do it *twice*.
2. **The verified-attention artefact (30 minutes watched + comprehension proved + voucher held) is a measurement product, not a media product** — which is rule #2 above, the survivor rule. If YourTal sells *that* into a research/performance budget rather than selling impressions into a media budget, it is on the survivor side of the line. Structure the product and the pricing around the artefact, not the video.
3. **Merchant-funded inventory means the reward cost is variable**, unlike Viggle's. Preserve that property absolutely: **never issue a point that is not matched by booked inventory.**

**The three things that will kill it, in order of likelihood:**

1. **Merchant second-campaign rate.** If it is below ~50%, the sales cost never amortises and you are Groupon in 2013 with worse distribution. This is knowable from campaign #1 and should gate every other decision.
2. **The pooled store's asymmetry.** Solve it before you build it, or do not pool at all — a per-merchant closed loop is worse for users and much safer for the business. Plenti is the proof.
3. **Points liability outrunning inventory.** The only defence is a hard mechanical binding between issuance and booked inventory, enforced in the ledger, not in policy.

### 13.4 The cheapest possible falsification

Do not build the platform to learn this. **Ten merchants, a landing page, a hosted video, a Google Form and hand-issued vouchers** answers the two questions that decide everything — *will users watch 30 minutes twice?* and *will a merchant fund a second campaign with their own money?* — in weeks, for approximately nothing. [`01-strategy-and-economics.md`](01-strategy-and-economics.md) §2.5 already says to do this. **This document is the argument for doing it before anything else.**
