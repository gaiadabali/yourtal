## 0. Verdict first

Watch-video-for-points-for-vouchers has been attempted at scale, with real money, at least a dozen times since 2011. **No pure-play attention-rewards company has reached durable standalone profitability.** The companies still alive in the adjacent space (Prodege, Mistplay) are not attention businesses — they are **performance-marketing and data businesses that use attention as a doorway.** That distinction is the whole document.

The three-sided structure YourTal proposes (earn at merchant A, spend at merchant B) has its own separate graveyard — **coalition loyalty** — and the largest US attempt, backed by American Express, died in three years.

## 1. Viggle — the most dangerous precedent

**Model:** launched 2012 by Robert F.X. Sillerman. Check in to a TV show by audio fingerprint, earn "Viggle Points", redeem for gift cards and merchandise. Later added ad-watching. Almost exactly YourTal's mechanic, minus the merchant-funded voucher.

| Metric                                             | Figure                                                                                                                             | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Revenue FY2015 (yr to 30 Jun)                      | **$25.6M** (+42% YoY)                                                                                                              | [Viggle FY2015 earnings release, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/725876/000072587615000071/a2015-6x30pressreleaseexh9.htm)                                                                                                     |
| Operating loss FY2015                              | **$76.4M**                                                                                                                         | same                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Net loss FY2015 / FY2014                           | **$78.5M / $68.4M**                                                                                                                | [Viggle FY2015 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000725876/000072587615000071/vggl-2015x630x10xk.htm)                                                                                                                          |
| Accumulated deficit at 30 Jun 2015                 | **~$367.4M**                                                                                                                       | same 10-K                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Net registered users                               | 9.52M (9.75M gross, 223k deactivated)                                                                                              | same 10-K                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Retail value of **all** rewards ever redeemed      | **$26.4M** over ~3.5 years                                                                                                         | FY2015 earnings release                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| "Cost of watchpoints and engagement points" FY2013 | **$8.5M** on revenue of $13.9M                                                                                                     | [FY2014 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000725876/000072587614000049/vggl-2014x630x10xk.htm)                                                                                                                                 |
| Same line FY2014                                   | $2.3M — but the fall came from **$2.3M of breakage re-estimates and $2.4M of cost-per-point re-estimates**, not efficiency         | same                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Auditor opinion                                    | **going-concern doubt**                                                                                                            | FY2015 10-K                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Sale price of the Viggle app (Dec 2015)            | **1.5M Perk shares ≈ $4.7M** + $1M loan; Perk assumed an **$8.7M points liability**                                                | [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20151214005637/en/Perk.com%C2%A0Inc.-Signs-Asset-Purchase-Agreement-to-Acquire-Viggle-App), [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000725876/000135448815005528/vggl_8k.htm) |
| Parent (renamed Function(x))                       | delisted from Nasdaq 2017; furloughed ~75% of staff Sept 2017 unable to pay wages; Sillerman later forced into personal bankruptcy | [IQ Magazine](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2019/07/robert-sillerman-fined-179000-sec-investment-fraud/)                                                                                                                                       |

**Why it died.** ~$367M of accumulated deficit against 9.5M registered users is **~$38.60 burned per registered user** — against a business that handed out $26.4M of rewards in its entire life. In its *best* year it made $25.6M and lost $78.5M. The points liability was material enough that the only way to show cost improvement was to **re-estimate breakage** — i.e. to book the assumption that users would never redeem.

**Lesson for YourTal.** (a) Points liability grows faster than revenue and is only flattered by breakage assumptions — exactly the assumptions an auditor or BI/ASIC will challenge. (b) Registered users are a worthless metric: 9.5M of them sold for $4.7M, or **$0.49 per registered user**. (c) A rewards app that merely loses money on rewards is survivable; Viggle's killer was that rewards + content + engineering + sales exceeded revenue by ~3x, permanently.

## 2. Perk.com / Perk TV — the best-executed version of this idea, and it still ended

**Model:** Perk TV, **Perk Pop Quiz** (literally quiz-for-points), Perk Word Search, Perk Browser. Watch short video, answer quizzes, redeem points for gift cards. Perk bought Viggle's app (2015) and AppRedeem (2015), consolidating almost the entire US rewarded-video category.

| Metric                      | Figure                                                                                      | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Q2 2016 revenue             | **$20.4M** (+64% YoY); net loss only **$0.5M** vs $15.8M prior year                         | [Perk Q2 2016, Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20160812005055/en/Perk-Reports-2016-Quarter-Financial-Results-Highlighted)                                                                |
| H1 2016 revenue             | **$37.7M** (+106%); net loss $2.8M                                                          | same                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Sale to RhythmOne, Jan 2017 | **$43.7M** for 100% — roughly **0.6x revenue**                                              | [RhythmOne F-4/A, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001713721/000119312518021752/d399085df4.htm)                                                                                                   |
| Fate                        | **All Perk apps shut down 1 Dec 2019**; points expired 20 Nov 2019                          | [Frugal for Less](https://www.frugalforless.com/perk-tv-review/), [AccessWire](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/computers-technology-and-internet/video-rewards-launch-spells-end-of-perk-tv-428562) |
| User earn rate              | a point ≈ **one tenth of a cent**; "a phone left running all night earned roughly 55 cents" | [Frugal for Less](https://www.frugalforless.com/perk-tv-review/)                                                                                                                                               |

**Why it ended.** Perk reached ~$75M annualised revenue and roughly breakeven — the best outcome anyone in this category achieved — and sold for a fire-sale adtech multiple. Its acquirer then switched it off. RhythmOne's FY2018 20-F describes Perk only as "supply" integrated into a programmatic platform, with **no mention of the consumer business at all** ([20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001713721/000143774918014094/rhyth20180713_20f.htm)).

**The fraud problem Perk proved.** Paying per view produced "phone farms" — racks of cheap handsets running video 24/7. Farmers with ~20 phones made **$50–100/month**; in 2018 there was a "very sharp downfall" in revenues as advertisers cracked down ([The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/phone-farmers-ad-fraud), [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-to-make-a-phone-farm/)).

**Lesson for YourTal.** The consumer rewards front-end proved worth _less than the ad supply it generated_. And **a quiz did not stop farming** — Perk Pop Quiz was quiz-gated and still farmed. Assume any comprehension check will be automated within months of the payout becoming worth automating. Budget for continuous adversarial work, not a launch-time anti-fraud feature.

## 4. Brave / BAT — 100M users, and it still retreated from paying them

**Model:** privacy browser pays users **70% of ad revenue** in BAT for viewing private ads ([Brave](https://brave.com/blog/brave-ads-on-android/)).

| Metric                      | Figure                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Source                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Brave MAU                   | **>100M**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | [Brave](https://brave.com/blog/100m-mau/)                                              |
| Advertisers                 | ~460+ named brands                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | [Brave](https://brave.com/blog/brave-ads-on-android/)                                  |
| Early desktop opt-in to ads | >40% in 2019 testing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/24/brave-ads)                              |
| BAT all-time high           | **$1.92**, 28 Nov 2021                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | [CoinLore](https://www.coinlore.com/coin/basic-attention-token)                        |
| BAT market cap 2025         | **~$115M**, rank ~#172 — **~96% below the 2021 peak market cap**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/currencies/basic-attention-token/)           |
| Brave's retreat (Feb 2023)  | **"virtual BAT" sunset.** Unverified users can no longer earn for themselves — only a "creator support only" mode. Stated reasons: abuse modelling assumed vBAT would expire; and vBAT is _"impossible to tell whether… lost to a discarded device, abandoned upon Brave uninstall, or lost due to profile deletion"_ | [Brave, "Important Changes to Brave Rewards"](https://brave.com/blog/rewards-changes/) |

**Read the retreat carefully.** The best-funded, best-distributed attention-rewards product in the world, with 100M users, **removed most users' ability to earn** because it could not control abuse or account for unredeemed balances. That is YourTal's exact problem set: fraud on the earn side, unaccountable liability on the spend side. Brave's solution was to make earning harder.

User-reported earnings fell from roughly $10/month to roughly $2/month over the same period — **anecdotal, forum-sourced** ([Brave Community](https://community.brave.app/t/bat-rewards-are-getting-lower/543161)); **no audited per-user earnings figure is published by Brave.**

**Lesson for YourTal.** Advertiser demand, not user supply, is the binding constraint. Brave had effectively unlimited user attention and could not fill it at a price that supported a meaningful per-user reward.

## 5. Crypto "to-earn" — the structural failure mode

| Project                           | Peak                                                                                                             | Collapse                                                                                 | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **StepN** (move-to-earn)          | ~700k+ MAU May 2022; **$122.5M profit in Q2 2022**                                                               | MAU to ~42k by early 2023 (**–94%**); Q3 profit **–90% QoQ**; GMT/GST **–90%+** from ATH | [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/consensus-magazine/2023/03/13/web3-exercise-app-stepn-crypto-rewards), [BanklessTimes](https://www.banklesstimes.com/news/2023/03/03/stepn-user-growth-disappoints-as-gst-and-gmt-prices-plunge/) |
| **Coinbase Earn** (learn-to-earn) | the category's one genuine success — a _customer-acquisition_ channel for a company with real downstream revenue | **discontinued 27 May 2025**                                                             | [Coinbase Help](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/getting-started/getting-started-with-coinbase/coinbase-earn-faq-and-terms)                                                                                                      |

**The pattern is arithmetic, not sentiment.** When the reward's value is set by demand for the reward _itself_ rather than by a third party buying something real, growth in earners dilutes the reward, the reward drops below the effort threshold, earners leave, and it drops further. CoinDesk's own verdict on the category: move-to-earn _"has a Ponzi-economics problem"_ ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/opinion/2023/03/13/move-to-earn-has-a-ponzi-problem-culture-week)).

**Lesson for YourTal.** YourTal's points are _not_ structurally Ponzi — they are backed by merchant-funded inventory at COGS, which is real value. **That holds only while inventory grows at least as fast as points issued.** The moment points outrun voucher supply, YourTal reproduces StepN's dynamics exactly: devaluation, then exit. **Points issuance must be mechanically bound to booked inventory, not to engagement targets.** If a growth lever can mint points without minting inventory, it is a time bomb.

## 6. Prodege (Swagbucks / InboxDollars / MyPoints) — the survivor, and what it actually sells

| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| Registered members               | **120M** across 7 properties                                                                                                         | [dot.LA](https://dot.la/prodege-unicorn-2656259289.html)                                                                                                                                        |
| Cumulative rewards paid          | **$1.8B**                                                                                                                            | same                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Revenue / registered member / yr | **~$2.50** (arithmetic: $300M ÷ 120M)                                                                                                | derived from the two figures above                                                                                                                                                              |
| Who pays                         | Walmart, Clorox, DoorDash, Target, Dollar Shave Club — **market-research and performance budgets**, plus ~5% commission on purchases | [dot.LA](https://dot.la/prodege-unicorn-2656259289.html)                                                                                                                                        |
| Roll-up history                  | InboxDollars (">$25M ARR" at 2019 acquisition), then Pollfish, BitBurst, AdGate Media                                                | [Prodege](https://www.prodege.com/resources/press-release/insights/prodege-llc-acquires-leading-consumer-rewards-site-inboxdollars-com/)                                                        |

**What they do differently — the most important table here.**

|                           | Viggle / Perk / YourTal-as-described             | Prodege                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product sold              | attention (impressions, completed views)         | **permissioned consumer data + measurable actions**                    |
| Buyer's budget line       | media                                            | **research + performance + trade**                                     |
| Buyer's alternative       | Meta, Google, TV                                 | Nielsen, Kantar, a CPA network                                         |
| Reward funded by          | a fixed cost paid per view regardless of outcome | **a share of a commission that only exists if a transaction happened** |
| Marginal cost of a reward | fixed, unavoidable                               | **variable, funded by the event it rewards**                           |

CEO Chuck Davis states the model plainly: _"We get the member — the consumer — to give us their permission to have a quid pro quo."_ Founder Josef Gorowitz: _"Brands buy into it — they're happy to share instead of putting it all into Google's profits."_ ([dot.LA](https://dot.la/prodege-unicorn-2656259289.html))

**Read the revenue figure honestly.** ~$2.50 per registered member per year is *not* a large number. Prodege survives on volume, near-zero marginal cost per member, and **acquisition of its own category** — it is a roll-up, not a startup. On that benchmark, YourTal at 750k Indonesian users grosses ~**$1.9M/year** — and Indonesian panel and CPA rates are a fraction of US rates. The [`01`](01-strategy-and-economics.md) §2.4 assumption of ~$0.55/user/month in Indonesia is **higher than Prodege's global average of ~$0.21/registered-member/month.** That assumption needs evidence before it carries a plan.

## 7. Mistplay — the other survivor, and why its trick does not transfer

**Model:** rewarded Android gaming. Users earn "units" for playtime and in-game achievements, redeem for gift cards. Game publishers pay.

| Metric        | Figure                                                                                         | Source                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Users         | 16M registered (2021); **>2M MAU across 9 markets** (current)                                  | [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mistplay-receives-majority-investment-to-scale-play-to-earn-platform/), [Mistplay](https://business.mistplay.com/advertisers/android) |
| Rewards paid  | $19M (2021) → **>$200M cumulative**                                                            | same two                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Revenue       | **$46.2M (2023)** — third-party estimate, **treat as unverified**; no audited figure published | [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/mistplay.com)                                                                                                                             |
| Ownership     | majority investment, GrowthCurve Capital, Dec 2021; **terms undisclosed**                      | [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mistplay-receives-majority-investment-to-scale-play-to-earn-platform/)                                                                |
| Pricing model | **not publicly disclosed**; markets on ROAS outcomes ("+43% ROAS", "300% install growth")      | [Mistplay](https://business.mistplay.com/advertisers/loyalty-program)                                                                                                               |

**Why it works and a generic attention app does not:** the advertised product _is itself a monetising machine_. A rewarded game install produces IAP revenue within days, attributable, on the advertiser's own ledger, at ~70%+ incremental margin. Mistplay can be paid out of a measurable LTV. **There is no equivalent instant, attributable, high-margin conversion event for a restaurant or a retailer.** A voucher redemption is the closest YourTal gets — but it lands days later, offline, at a merchant who may or may not scan it correctly. **Closing that attribution loop is YourTal's hardest and most valuable engineering problem, not the video player.**

**The counter-evidence to confront.** AppsFlyer benchmarks 30-day retention at **1.1%** for incent-heavy networks versus **2.4–4.4%** for gaming apps generally ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/blog/mobile-marketing/rewarded-advertising-good-bad-ugly/)). Rewarded users are measurably worse users. The one serious academic counterweight — Chiong & Yang, 1M impressions, three quasi-experimental designs — finds incentivised advertising **lowers CTR but raises install rate**, and lifts publisher revenue by **$3.10 per 1,000 impressions**, attributed to reduced ad annoyance ([arXiv:1709.00197](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00197)). So rewarded attention is real and monetisable _at the impression level_. It is the **downstream value** that collapses.

**No public figure found** for CashTree's current MAU/revenue, CashPop or BuzzBreak funding, or a TikTok Lite rewards launch/shutdown in Indonesia.

### 8.2 The Indonesian regulatory ceiling — this is the section that matters most

**In Indonesia the binding regulator is OJK, not Bank Indonesia.** No BI rule governing loyalty points or capping promo spend was found; PBI 20/6/2018 governs e-money issuers, not points schemes ([bi.go.id](https://www.bi.go.id/elicensing/helps/PBI-200618%20UE.pdf)). The live test is **whether the reward is funded by recruitment/deposits rather than by goods or services sold** — if so, OJK/Satgas PASTI calls it a _money game_ and Kominfo blocks the app. In 2022 alone the task force shut **21 illegal entities, 16 of them money games** ([Kompas](https://money.kompas.com/read/2022/02/20/170000826/daftar-21-investasi-ilegal-yang-baru-ditutup-satgas-waspada-investasi-)). OJK also warns it **never publishes any list of "official" money-making apps** ([OJK](https://ojk.go.id/id/Publikasi/Info-Hoax/Pages/OJK-Tidak-Pernah-Mengeluarkan-Daftar-Aplikasi-Penghasil-Uang.aspx)). **PSE registration with Komdigi is not an OJK licence** — a distinction rewarded apps routinely blur.

**The squeeze, stated plainly:** make the reward large enough to change behaviour and you must fund it from recruitment → money game → blocked. Make it small enough to self-fund and it is worth a fraction of minimum wage. **Jakarta's UMP 2026 is Rp5,729,876/month** ([Kepgub 1142/2025](https://m.beritajakarta.id/read/149722/naik-617-persen-ump-dki-jakarta-2026-ditetapkan-rp5729876)); at the statutory 173-hour divisor ([PP 36/2021](https://mekari.com/blog/cara-menghitung-upah-per-jam/)) that is **~Rp33,120/hour** _(our arithmetic, not a published figure)_. **YourTal's escape from this squeeze is that the reward is funded by merchant COGS, not recruitment — that is the plan's single most important legal and economic property, and it must never be compromised by a growth promotion that mints points from nothing.**

### 8.4 "Bakar uang": the cleanest proof that promo-acquired users do not retain

- **GoTo cut Rp9.0 trillion of incentives and product marketing in FY2023 (–38%).** Q3 2023 GTV fell **6% group-wide, –12% on-demand, –11% e-commerce**; Jan–Sep 2023 Tokopedia GTV **–9.31%**, Gojek **–8.47%**. Management's own words: _"The decrease in GTV is due to a reduction in unprofitable consumers as a result of significant incentive reductions over the past year."_ ([GoTo Q3 2023](https://www.gotocompany.com/en/news/press/goto-group-moves-towards-growth-and-improves-profitability-as-company-reports-2023-third-quarter-results), [Databoks/Katadata](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/market/statistics/dcbd70a565003ea/gojek-and-tokopedia-transaction-volumes-decline-through-q3-2023-what-are-the-causes))
- **Sea/Shopee, Q4 2022:** e-commerce sales & marketing **–54.9% YoY** ($840.7M → $379.4M); **gross orders fell from 2.0bn to 1.7bn, ~–15% YoY** in the same quarter ([Sea Q4/FY2022, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1703399/000110465923029545/tm238717d1_ex99-1.htm)).
- **OVO:** Lippo's Mochtar Riady, Nov 2019 — _"We're burning money."_ Lippo was putting in roughly **US$50M a month** and sold two-thirds of its stake ([e27](https://e27.co/were-burning-money-lippo-group-founder-mochtar-riady-selling-70-per-cent-stake-in-the-omnipresent-e-wallet-ovo-20191129/)).
- **50.2%** of Indonesian internet users aged 16–64 name **free-shipping promos** as their primary reason for shopping online ([Databoks/GWI](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/datapublish/2021/11/22/promo-gratis-ongkir-jadi-alasan-utama-pengguna-internet-belanja-online)).

**This is the direct answer to "investor-funded voucher inventory breaks the cold start."** It will. It will also manufacture exactly the cohort GoTo paid Rp9T to shed. The number that matters is not how many users the subsidy buys — it is **how many remain after it stops.** Instrument that from week one.

### 8.5 Groupon and the deal model in Indonesia: three owners, then voided vouchers

Disdus (2010) → **acquired by Groupon Apr 2011** → Groupon Indonesia stops shopping deals 2015 → **sold to KFit Jun 2016 for equity, price undisclosed** despite >1M subscribers and >15,000 merchants (Groupon took KFit stock rather than cash — a near-zero valuation signal) → rebranded **Fave** → **bought by Pine Labs Apr 2021 for US$45M** → **Fave exits Indonesia 1 Apr 2023; all purchased vouchers void from 1 June 2023.** ([Kontan](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/masuk-pasar-indonesia-groupon-akuisisi-disdus.com-1), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/06/05/groupon-is-selling-its-indonesia-business-to-fitness-membership-startup-kfit/), [TechCrunch/Pine Labs](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/13/pine-labs-acquires-southeast-asian-startup-fave-for-45-million/embed/), [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/fave-abruptly-exits-indonesian-market-as-it-looks-to-focus-on-other-regions))

**Ensogo** (ex-LivingSocial SEA, ASX-listed, Catcha Group) is the hardest set of numbers in the region: FY2015 operating deficit **AUD 78.2M**, share price **AUD 12.47 (Mar 2014) → AUD 0.65 (Jun 2016), ~–95%**, headcount 600 → <300 → zero when it **shut all SEA and Hong Kong operations overnight on 21 June 2016**, leaving thousands of Thai merchants unpaid for services already rendered and consumers holding vouchers merchants refused to honour ([e27](https://e27.co/publically-listed-e-commerce-platform-ensogo-is-imploding-20160621), [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/technology/137231-ensogo-shut-down-southeast-asian-marketplaces-lay-off-staff/), [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/1016137/ensogo-closes-e-commerce-operation-in-se-asia)).

**Lesson for YourTal.** The prepaid-voucher failure mode in SEA is not a quiet wind-down — it is **merchants refusing to honour outstanding vouchers while the operator is still nominally alive.** YourTal's outstanding points and vouchers are a consumer liability in two jurisdictions. Segregate the funds, publish the expiry terms, and assume a regulator will one day ask where the float is.
