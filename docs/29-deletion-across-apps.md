# YourTal — Deleting an account across the three apps

**Date:** 2026-11-03 · **Status:** Proposed, for the lead's review. Designed for group tasks 3.7 and 3.9.f (YourTal 14.16.e). The founder's choices are in §14; until they are answered, the build uses the recommended option.
**Builds on:** [`26`](26-sign-in-with-yourtal.md) §2 (the event contract) and §3.5 (the copy), F88 (escrow on deletion), F108 and F124 (FTR behind its switch, no FTR staging), F111–F113 (teens, link codes), YourTal 1.20, 1.24 and 5.1.h (the deletion handlers).
**Feeds:** group 3.7.a/b, 3.9.f, 3.10.f and 8.3.a; YourTal 14.16.d/e.

When a YourTal account is deleted, every app the person signed in to with YourTal gets a signed event and erases what it holds within 72 hours. When an app's access ends (an adult removes it, a guardian withdraws, a parent ends a teen's invite), the app ends the person's sessions; a teen's space is erased after 30 days. YourTal's deletion, guardian and data-request flows stay as they are: a new `connected_apps` data domain writes the events in the same transaction that erases the person's app links, and the worker delivers them.

## 1. Decisions

| Question               | Answer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| How apps are told      | **Security Event Tokens** (RFC 8417) pushed by HTTP POST (RFC 8935), as docs/26 §2.1 chose. Not OpenID back-channel logout: it says "this YourTal session ended", carries no deletion meaning and no delivery guarantee, and apps run their own sessions (docs/26 §1.4; it is off in the provider, §1.1). Not an HMAC webhook: a secret per app to hand over (FTR is another company's) and rotate, and whoever holds it can forge. A SET is signed with the OIDC key apps already verify, and no app can forge one for another |
| The events             | docs/26 §2.2, unchanged: `account-deleted`; `access-ended` with `reason` and `erase_data`. `became-adult` ships with 3.10 on the same pipe                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Where they are written | Two SQL functions, `oidc.forget_subject` and `oidc.end_app_access`, called inside the transaction that makes the change: one implementation for the API's callers and the worker's sweep                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Who sends              | The YourTal worker, every minute, through a new `app_events` driver boundary: simulated in dev and CI, live on staging (docs/26 §2.4)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Clocks                 | Sessions end within 5 minutes; data is erased within 72 hours of `account-deleted`; a teen's space 30 days after access ends (F111)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| What apps keep         | Only records the app itself must keep, cut off from the person. No app keeps a customer's own records for them after they ask for deletion (§14 Q1)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| SnapApp workspaces     | Erase the workspaces the person runs alone. In shared ones, remove the person and null their attribution; if they were the only owner, the earliest admin becomes owner (§14 Q2)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

Defaults taken (no founder input needed): **D1** FTR acts inline and answers 202 only after acting: it has no job runner, so YourTal's retries are its queue. **D2** SnapApp's inbox is its `jobs` table, keyed by `jti`. **D3** an app ends only the sessions opened before the event's `iat`, so a late retry cannot end a newer session. **D4** a failed event no longer holds back later ones for the same person and app; otherwise docs/26 §2.4's order stands. **D5** a resend is a runbook SQL line until a staff page exists. **D6** an account linked to YourTal only for points, by link code (group 2.5), is not a YourTal sign-in: YourTal revokes the link (`partner_links`) and the app account stays.

## 3. The flow

**Deletion.** The person deletes in Me, or the guardian uses the delete link. `executeDeletion` reaches `connected_apps`, which calls `oidc.forget_subject`. In one transaction it writes one `account-deleted` row for each app the person has a `sub` in, ends the access of every teen the person invited (`inviter_deleted`), and erases the person's `oidc.subject`, `app_consent`, `teen_invite`, `sign_in_log` and provider records. Within a minute the worker signs each row and posts it. SnapApp stores the event as a job, records the `sub` as erased, revokes every session and answers 202; its worker erases the person. FTR ends the sessions, records the `sub`, runs `eraseAccount` and answers 202. YourTal marks each row delivered.

**Ended access.** An adult removes an app in Me → Linked apps (`user_removed`), a guardian withdraws (`guardian_withdrew`), a parent ends the invite (`parent_ended`, 14.16.d), or a teen removes SnapApp (`teen_removed`). `oidc.end_app_access` ends the consent, appends a `withdrawn` consent record naming the app, ends the teen's invite and writes one `access-ended` row. The app ends the sessions opened before `iat`. With `erase_data: false` the app account stays and the person comes back by agreeing again. With `true` (teens only) SnapApp keeps the space 30 days, a new invite restores it, and then it is erased (F111). A teen whose own account is deleted gets `account-deleted`: 72 hours, no hold, since no invite can bring back an erased `sub`.

**How the guardian and data-request flow hooks in (3.9.f).** The flows do not change. (1) `DELETE /api/me` and (2) the guardian's delete link both run `executeDeletion`, so the new domain covers both. (3) The guardian's withdrawal calls `oidc.end_app_access` inside its existing transaction. (4) The data export lists the person's apps, and its `otherDataDomains` picks up the new domain. (5) The deletion report gains a `connected_apps` line.

## 4. Event shape and transport

### 4.1 The wire

As docs/26 §2.3: `POST` with `Content-Type: application/secevent+jwt` and a compact JWS. The header is `{"typ":"secevent+jwt","alg":"RS256","kid":…}`. The claims are `iss`, `aud` (the receiving client), `iat` (when the change happened), `jti` (a uuid), `sub_id` `{"format":"iss_sub", iss, sub}` and exactly one event in `events`. A retry resends the same `jti` and `iat`, signed with the current key.

### 4.2 Sending (Y4)

`apps/worker/src/jobs/app-event-delivery.ts` runs every minute. It claims up to 100 due rows with a 2-minute lease on `next_attempt_at`, oldest `iat` first, and skips a row while an earlier one for the same client and `sub` is still retrying. It builds the claims from the row and the configured issuer, signs them with the first key in `OIDC_JWKS_PATH` (jose `SignJWT`) and posts through the `app_events` driver: live, with a 10 s timeout and `redirect: "error"`; simulated, with no call and a 202.

| The app answers                        | YourTal                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `202`                                  | Sets `delivered_at`                                                                                                  |
| `400 {"err":"invalid_key"}`            | Retries: the app may hold an old JWKS                                                                                |
| Any other `400`                        | Sets `failed_at`; error log `app_event.refused`                                                                      |
| `429`, `5xx`, a timeout, anything else | Retries after 1, 5, 15 and 30 minutes, then hourly; after 7 days sets `failed_at`; error log `app_event.undelivered` |

Logs carry the `jti`, the client and the event type, never the token. The job also purges rows delivered more than 30 days ago. Every 30 minutes it runs the sweep: for each `oidc.subject` older than 10 minutes whose `identity.user_profile` is gone, it calls `oidc.forget_subject`, so a deletion whose `connected_apps` step failed still reaches the apps. A resend is `UPDATE oidc.event_outbox SET failed_at = NULL, attempts = 0, next_attempt_at = now() WHERE jti = …`, or `WHERE client_id = … AND iat >= <backup time>` after an app restores a backup.

### 4.3 Receiving (S2, F1)

Each app verifies with jose against YourTal's JWKS, found through discovery: `typ` `secevent+jwt`, `RS256` only, `iss` the configured issuer, `aud` its own client id, `iat` at most 5 minutes ahead and at most 30 days old, exactly one event, and `sub_id.iss` equal to the issuer. On an unknown `kid` it refetches the JWKS once, then answers `400 invalid_key`. It answers within 10 seconds. Other `400`s carry RFC 8935's `err` (`invalid_request`, `invalid_issuer`, `invalid_audience`, `authentication_failed`).

Idempotency: SnapApp keys on the `jti` (its job id) and FTR on the `sub`; sessions end only if opened before `iat` (D3). After `account-deleted` the `sub` goes into `yourtal_erased_subjects`: every later event for it is a no-op, and its sign-in is refused for good (docs/26 §2.2: "never recreate this `sub`"). An unknown `sub` is recorded and answered 202, and so is an unknown event type. The endpoint works whenever the issuer and client id are set, with sign-in switched off too: turning sign-in off must not stop deletions.

## 5. YourTal design

- **Receivers (Y2).** `packages/contracts/src/oidc/event-receivers.ts` lists `{sector, clientId, endpoint, environments}` for `snapapp-staging` and `ftr`. `clients.ts` takes `eventsEndpoint` from it, and its test fails if a sign-in sector has no receiver. The API and the worker both read it.
- **Functions (Y1: one add-only migration and atlas.sum).** `oidc.end_app_access(user_id, sector, client_id, reason) → boolean`. `reason` is one of docs/26 §2.2's six; `erase_data` is false for `user_removed` and `security` and true otherwise. It ends the live `app_consent` row, appends an `identity.consent_record` (`withdrawn`, source = the reason, `audience` = the sector, jurisdiction from `user_profile.region`), ends the person's live invite in that sector and writes one `access-ended` row for their `sub`. With no live consent it writes nothing and returns false. `oidc.forget_subject(user_id, receivers jsonb) → integer` ends every accepted, live invite the person made (`end_app_access(teen, …, 'inviter_deleted')`), writes one `account-deleted` row for each `oidc.subject` row whose sector has a receiver, then deletes the person's rows in `subject`, `app_consent`, `teen_invite` (as inviter or teen), `sign_in_log` and `oidc.model` (`payload->>'accountId'`). A second call finds nothing. Both are plain functions: `yourtal_app` already holds every grant. Add an index on `event_outbox (client_id, sub, iat) WHERE delivered_at IS NULL`.
- **The domain (Y2).** `connected_apps` in `dsar.ts` (`erase`, owner ops, service oidc); its handler `forgetConnectedApps` in `packages/db/src/dsar-handlers.ts`, registered by `postgresHandlers` with the receivers, which `deletionHandlers` (`apps/api/src/shared/dsar/deletion-handlers.ts:50-59`) passes in. Also correct the identity domain's stale "YourtalID (Zitadel)".
- **Guardian withdrawal (Y2).** Inside `revoke-guardian-consent.use-case.ts`'s transaction, when the row transitions, call `end_app_access` for each receiver with `guardian_withdrew`. Today it finds nothing, because no teen can reach an app before 3.10, so 3.10 never has to touch the guardian flow.
- **Removing an app (Y3).** `GET /api/me/connected-apps` lists live consents with the app, operator and date from the registry. `DELETE /api/me/connected-apps/:app` ends one (`user_removed`, or `teen_removed` for a teen; idempotent). Cerbos `me` actions `view_connected_apps` and `remove_connected_app`. `POST /api/me/consents` refuses `sister_app_profile_sharing`: only the consent screen and this removal record it. The data export gains `connectedApps` (app, scopes, granted, ended; never the `sub`). 14.16.d's invite `end` calls `end_app_access(…, 'parent_ended')`.
- **Sender (Y4).** §4.2. A new boundary `app_events` in `boundary.ts` (`APP_EVENTS_DRIVER`; live needs `OIDC_JWKS_PATH`), and a third staging exception in `driver-mode.ts`: it can reach only the receivers in the registry. - **Web (Y5).** Me → Linked apps gains "Apps you sign in to with YourTal", each with Remove and docs/26 §3.5's dialogs, the teen one saying the space is deleted after 30 days. ## 6. SnapApp design

- **S1 (migration 0043 or the next free, add-only).** `yourtal_erased_subjects (sub text PRIMARY KEY, erased_at)`. And `erase_person(user_id) → integer`, SECURITY DEFINER in the 0039 idiom, run by the worker:
  1. For each workspace where the person is owner or admin and no other owner or admin remains, erase the workspace in dependency order: the rows that reference its documents without `ON DELETE`, then its captures (past the RESTRICT; documents cascade), then the tenant (everything else cascades). Enqueue a `storage_purge` job for `{tenantId}/` in the same transaction.
  2. Where the person was the only owner and an admin remains, the earliest admin becomes owner.
  3. Delete `mail_deliveries` to their address, then the user: sessions, links, memberships, the staff row and the points ledger cascade, and attribution becomes NULL. A second call finds nothing.
     A teen space is erased with the teen, so 3.10.b must make the teen its owner.
- **S2 (the endpoint and the job).** An `application/secevent+jwt` text parser in `main.ts` (16 KB). `POST /v1/yourtal/events` verifies (§4.3, audience `YOURTAL_OIDC_SERVER_CLIENT_ID`), then in one transaction: a `jobs` row (`id = jti`, kind `yourtal_event`, `tenant_id` NULL so it survives the erasure, `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`) carrying the raw SET; every session revoked (`account-deleted`) or the sessions opened before `iat` (`access-ended`). ## 10. Risks

- A failed `connected_apps` step does not stop the rest of the deletion, and nothing reads `complete`. The sweep (§4.2) closes this the way 5.1.h did for Stripe. The general gap, a person told "deleted" when a domain failed, predates this design: `account.controller.ts:126` could log `complete: false`.
- YourTal staging posts to FTR production (F124). Only FTR accounts linked during switch-on tests carry a staging `sub`, so nothing else can match.
- An app down for more than 7 days: the events stop with an error log and wait for a manual resend. FTR's payout-in-batch case can pass 72 hours while money is in flight; that exception is written down.
- Erasing the workspaces a person runs alone also removes records a bookkeeper or member used (Q2). One wrong join in `erase_person` would erase another member's records, hence Opus on S1 and its test matrix.
- `oidc.model` is searched by a JSON field with no index. That is fine at today's size, since the hourly purge keeps the table small.
