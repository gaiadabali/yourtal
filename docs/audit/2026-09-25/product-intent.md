## 1. The product in plain terms

A business uploads a 1–30 minute video (1–5 min is the workhorse, per docs/16 J9 and docs/23 §1.1), writes 3–5 questions, and **pre-buys YourTal Points** to fund the viewers' reward. A signed-in user watches **the whole video** and answers the questions. Only then does the server grant the number of points that business chose (O-1, O-4, O-5). Points sit in a holdback, then buy vouchers (and later merchandise) from **one pooled store**. The platform sets store prices in points: `points_price = (S/B) × m`. The user shows a rotating QR at the merchant's till, or the merchant's checkout calls authorize/capture, and the voucher is redeemed. Between the two ends YourTal clears the money: it collects `P_issue` per point from the funder, pays the supplier's settlement value `S` when an item is redeemed, and keeps the spread plus breakage. Anonymous visitors can watch any campaign that has opted in, but they can never earn (Open Viewing). There is no cash-out, users cannot buy points, and points cannot be transferred.

### 2.2 Core journeys the v1 must run end to end, in both regions

1. **Business onboarding.** The owner registers with email and password, creates a business in a region (the region is immutable), uploads KYB (ABN for AU, NIB for ID; manual Ops review at v1), and invites team members by role.
2. **Partner funding.** Finance buys a block of points through the simulated Stripe (AUD) or Xendit (IDR) driver. Cash posts to the segregated reserve (`FundReserve`) and an allocation is created _from that purchase_. Billing shows the purchase, drawdown and remainder.
3. **Campaign authoring.** The Marketer uploads a video (direct to object storage) and a transcode job produces HLS capped at 720p. The Marketer then writes the question bank (at least 3× the number asked, at most 5 asked, 1 per 5 minutes, no PII). The reward set per completion is drawn from an allocation, plus the accuracy-bonus rule, targeting (geo, age band, declared interest), schedule, and the Open-View opt-in. The campaign goes through the simulated automated screen, then a human moderator, then goes live.
4. **Supplier listing.** The Merchandiser declares face value, `S`, stock, locations, channel, transferability, partial-redemption policy and expiry. **The platform computes the price.** Any downward change to `S` needs two-person approval with no threshold (docs/17 §2.1).
5. **Consumer signup.** The user arrives from `/` or a shared campaign link. They choose a region, which is fixed at registration, and a language, which **defaults to en-AU**. They register with email and password, verify the email, give per-purpose consents, pick interests, and land back where they started (`returnTo`).
6. **Earn.** The card states "duration · up to N pts · ~MB · merchant", and those terms are locked at start. The server tracks playback coverage through signed per-session segment URLs and checkpoint tokens at randomised times. Once coverage is full, the server serves the questions (per-user subset, shuffled options, timers) and scores the answers. The Reward Engine then grants the campaign's partner-set amount, the points appear as **pending with an unlock date**, and the UI displays the figure the server returned.
7. **Burn.** The user opens a listing and sees the terms. The price is locked for 15 minutes. The saga then runs: reserve stock → burn _available_ points → mint voucher (code hashed and encrypted) → voucher appears in the Wallet with a signed rotating QR that still works offline.
8. **Redeem.** Store staff unlock the device with a PIN and scan or type the code. The device calls authorize (amount + order ref), then capture, and the receipt shows on both sides. Online merchants make the same two calls via HMAC.
9. **Settle.** Accrual runs nightly. Each week every partner gets a statement (issued vs honoured, netted to one figure) that reproduces from ledger entries. Finance approves, and a simulated payout runs after the dispute window.
10. **Lifecycle.** Warnings go out before points expire (12 months after last activity) and before vouchers expire. When expiry happens, the ledger posts `ExpirePoints` as breakage.
11. **Open View.** An anonymous visitor can watch the full video. The page shows the foregone reward and offers sign-up; nothing is ever claimable.
12. **snap-app receipt earn.** The snap-app server calls the earn API with a receipt hash. The resulting points are funded by group-marketing **cash into the reserve** (F3/K6).
13. **Support and risk.** If a voucher fails at the till, the user is refunded in points immediately and YourTal recovers the value at settlement (K13). If an account is suspected of fraud, it is suspended and its balance escrowed, never silently zeroed (docs/18 §5).
14. **Privacy.** Consent can be withdrawn and takes effect on the next recompute. DSAR and account deletion run through `packages/consent`.

## 5. Economic invariants

1. **One-way valve** (docs/01 §3; docs/24 red lines 3–4). Users never buy points. There is no published cash rate. Points are not transferable. Points expire. Nothing converts cash to points. No cash-out: `cashOutEnabled: false` (`packages/jurisdiction/src/policy-data.ts:23,39`) is locked by a test. **There is no code-level prohibition on a user-facing purchase path** (docs/24 audit row 4).
2. **Pricing** (docs/09 §4.1; docs/16 B1/K1/K3; docs/25 §2). `points_price = ceil((S / B) × m)`. `S` is declared by the supplier and must be ≤ face value. `B` is set by the platform per currency: **ID IDR 6/pt, AU 3¢/pt**. `m ∈ [0.80, 1.25]`, fixed at 1.00 for launch. Rounding always goes up. `B < P_issue` is a CHECK constraint. **Suppliers never set the price.** A price is locked for 15 minutes once shown. There is no per-user price discrimination. `B` never reaches a client or a response (docs/25 §5 says this breach is live today).
3. **Spread** (docs/07 §2.1). `P_issue − B`. ID is 9 − 6 = IDR 3/pt (33%). **AU `P_issue` is undecided** (docs/25 §2 lists only an ID row), and AU is the primary market.
4. **Solvency** (docs/09 §5; docs/16 K7). Coverage = Reserve ÷ (points outstanding × B) must stay ≥ 1.0. Below 1.2 it alerts, below 1.1 promotional faucets stop, and below 1.0 unfunded issuance is blocked. Reserve = Σ partner purchases (at P_issue) + Σ platform-funded grants as real cash − Σ settlements paid − Σ operating draws (which must be 0). It is computed per currency from projections, never from a stored total.
5. **K6.** Every point not paid for by a business (streak, referral, promotion, goodwill, snap-app) is backed by a real cash transfer into the reserve at the moment it is issued. The code does not enforce this today: `CreateAllocation("marketing", …)` needs no cash (`reward/setup.go:46–62`). A purchase's cash/points ratio is recorded, not checked against B (`reward/purchase.go:18–21`).
6. **Funding.** Pre-purchase only (J1). Every campaign must be reward-funded (D5). Drawdown is atomic and hard-stops at zero (`engine.go` `DrawDownAllocation`).
7. **Reward rule.** All-or-nothing: full playback coverage plus answered questions (O-1, O-4), decided only on the server (O-5). The amount is the partner's per-campaign choice (founder 2026-09-22, migration `20260922020000`). The terms shown at entry are the terms honoured (docs/06 §4.2). AU ceiling: under AUD 5 per 20 minutes, i.e. under 167 pts (docs/25 §8), and that is a ceiling, not a wage. **The fixed 2,400 pts in `taxonomy.go:89` is AUD 72 at B(AU).**
8. **Holdback** (B7; docs/22 §7). 72/48/24/0 h by trust tier. Pending points cannot be burned. 72 h is the floor for new web accounts.
9. **Redemption.** Capture ≤ authorize. Holds expire after 15 minutes. There is no balance lookup. `redeemed` is terminal and there are no refunds (P-1). If a merchant fails to honour a voucher, the user gets points back instantly (K13). Settlement is held across the dispute window (K14). The platform bears the loss on fraudulent vouchers (B8).
10. **Commerce saga** (docs/07 §4.2). reserve → debit → mint → confirm. Every step is compensated on failure, and points are never debited before stock is reserved.
11. **Isolation.** Points, reserves, float and clearing are all country-scoped. There is no cross-border redemption (B6; docs/07 §2.4). Float is segregated (docs/07 §2.3).
12. **Units** (T-1). IDR exponent 0, AUD exponent 2, integers only. Each driver declares the unit it uses, with no IDR default.
13. **Governance.** Any downward change to `S` needs two-person approval with no threshold (docs/17 §2.1). A change to `B` needs two people, is announced in advance, and never publishes `B` itself (docs/25 §4). No business role can grant points (docs/17 §2.1). Statements reproduce from ledger entries (docs/07 §2.2). `last_activity_at` is written in the same transaction as the earn or burn (docs/25 §3).

## 6. What docs/23 and docs/24 say must be true at launch

**docs/24 red lines** (`:73–83`): (1) no prediction/guessing games; (2) no chance prizes; (3) no cash withdrawal; (4) no user purchase of points; (5) no forward breakage or redemption claims to merchants (Scoopon); (6) no sensitive-category inference; (7) no physical-goods merchant without a verified NIB; (8) no charitable funds held; (9) no ID users before PSE registration; (10) no user-level advertiser export; (11) **no real personal data on Helios** until its location is documented _and_ APP 8 is handled for AU data (uncommitted text). Only #2 and #3 are enforced in code. The v1 must turn #1, #4, #6, #10 and #11 into code-level controls with tests, following the jurisdiction pattern, and must avoid #7 and #8 by scope.

**docs/24 go-live gates, non-engineering:** the founder personally re-executes the risk acceptance before the first real user (`:165`); minimum age is cited for both markets (ID-12: 21 is safer; AU-9); PSE registration for ID (ID-6); purpose-specific consent that is live before 16 Jan 2027 (ID-7); a rehearsed 72 h breach runbook (ID-8/AU-6); a counsel review of the register before the AU launch (`:151`); entity formation with a notaris (ID) and a Pty Ltd (AU).

**docs/23 must-be-trues:** fraud defence sits after the earn (vesting, caps, cross-account detection, trust-tiered reward mix) rather than at the front door (§1.0). Funders see attribution, e.g. "your points bought N views and M of your own vouchers redeemed", and rewards bias toward the funder's own inventory (§1.0b, Plenti). The format defaults to 60–180 s (§1.1). Realised value is measured, with low-minimum, high-utility rewards (§1.3). The deciding KPIs are the redeemer 90-day full-price return rate and the merchant second-campaign rate, not the redemption rate (§1.2, §1.4). Settlement is manual below 30 partners (§2.3). One deployment region with schema-level isolation (§2.4). The honest entry card is the load-bearing control on the reward framing, and **Australian reaction sessions are required before the AU launch** (§2.6). Open-view and rewarded metrics are separated structurally (3.3). D1/D7/D30 retention and support cost are modelled (3.6, 3.7). docs/21 §11 (TikTok Lite) adds a consumer-harm risk assessment, hard age gating and a daily earn cap before launch.

## 7. Definition of finished and deferred scope

### v1 is finished when all of the following are true (engineering, on Helios, simulated drivers, seeded/internal data only)

1. **No mocks in the running product.** The Helios build serves every surface from the live API. `mock` survives only in tests and Storybook, and a test fails if a production build resolves `mock`.
2. **English by default.** Every surface uses one i18n system, with `en-AU` the default and `id-ID` complete (parity test). Language is independent of region. There are no hard-coded strings (today there are four mechanisms: next-intl, `merchant-copy.ts`, `me-i18n.ts`, and inline strings such as "Tukar Poin" in `store/[listingId]/redeem/page.tsx:24,53`).
3. **Journeys 1–14 run** in AU (AUD) and ID (IDR) as Playwright e2e against the deployed stack.
4. **Engines meet §3's minimum**, each with invariant tests, and §5's invariants are all enforced in code. In particular: reward amount = partner config; solvency gate at 1.1 and 1.0; K6 cash-backed marketing allocations; the price computed server-side only; `B` absent from client bundles; answer keys absent from the browser.
5. **The full stack runs on Helios.** web, api, ledger, voucher, postgres, valkey, cerbos, minio and the worker, not the web app alone (`.gaiadeploy.yml` today deploys only `pm2 yourtal-web`). Pull-deploy, a rehearsed rollback, backups/PITR, and "hasn't run" paging are all in place.
6. **Staff console at minimum.** Moderation queue, listing/KYB approval, point-purchase recording, statements, coverage and daily-economy view, suspend/escrow, goodwill credit, and kill switches.
7. **Redesigned UI** on one design system that meets the docs/08 §3.1 budgets on mid-tier Android.
8. **Red lines reachable at v1 are code-enforced**, and red line 11 is a boot-time config assertion.
